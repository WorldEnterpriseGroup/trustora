When an international employee needs an office, the question is often treated as a real-estate problem. That is too narrow. The better question is: what environment lets this person do the work safely, securely, and with the right level of focus?

An EoR provider may help recommend serviced offices, coworking options, labs, meeting rooms, or other local arrangements. That support does not turn the provider into a landlord or a substitute for a workplace, safety, legal, or tax adviser. It does make the employee experience more complete.

## Start with the work

The workspace needs of a quantum researcher may differ from those of a product engineer or an executive building a new regional team. Before looking at addresses, define the operating requirements:

- privacy and confidentiality for the role;
- secure connectivity, access controls, and device handling;
- meeting, collaboration, and quiet-focus needs;
- equipment, power, lab, or prototyping requirements;
- accessibility, transport, and reasonable flexibility;
- a credible local experience for the employee and their manager.

The office should be evaluated as part of the job design, not chosen from a glossy list of amenities.

## Six questions for a recommendation

1. Can the employee work there without exposing sensitive information?
2. Does the connectivity and access model fit the role?
3. Is the space physically accessible and practical for the person using it?
4. Can the employee alternate between focus and collaboration?
5. Are the local responsibilities and incident routes clear?
6. What happens if the role, team, or location changes?

Workplace guidance varies by country and by the actual arrangement. Our job is to make the decision more informed, document the assumptions, and route specialist questions to the right local adviser.
