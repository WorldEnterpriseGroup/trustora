Working from home is not one universal arrangement. It might mean a dedicated room, a shared apartment, a temporary setup, or a hybrid week that changes over time. The employer’s task is to create a reasonable, secure, and supportable way for the employee to do the work.

The WHO and ILO identify physical, mental, and social dimensions to healthy telework. That is a useful frame for EoR conversations because it prevents “remote” from becoming shorthand for “the employee handles everything.”

## Four layers of a strong setup

**Physical.** Desk, chair, screen position, lighting, power, noise, and the practical ability to work without avoidable strain. Local health and safety duties differ, so the checklist should be adapted to the location.

**Technical.** A device, connectivity, identity, access, updates, support, and a clear path when something fails. The employee should know what is supplied, what is approved, and who owns the next step.

**Secure.** Privacy at home, safe handling of company information, device protection, and proportionate monitoring. Security should reduce exposure without turning the home into a surveillance zone.

**Human.** Working-time boundaries, manager contact, team rituals, quiet focus, social connection, and a way to raise a problem early. Remote work is a relationship design question as much as an equipment question.

## A small test for readiness

Ask the employee: “If your laptop, internet, workspace, privacy, or working hours create a problem next week, do you know who will help and what happens next?” If the answer is no, the remote setup is not finished.
