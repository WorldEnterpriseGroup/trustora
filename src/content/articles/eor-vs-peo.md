EoR and PEO are both used to describe outsourced employment support. That is where the similarity ends.

The useful comparison is not “which acronym is better?” It is: who owns the local employing entity, which responsibilities are shared, and what stage of expansion is the company in?

## The central difference

An Employer of Record model is typically used when a company wants to employ someone in a country where it does not yet have its own local entity. The EoR is the local employer named in the employment arrangement, while the client company directs the work.

A Professional Employer Organization relationship is commonly associated with co-employment. The client company usually already has an employing entity, and the PEO shares or supports specified employer responsibilities under the applicable model.

The exact legal and operational structure varies by jurisdiction and provider. The labels are starting points for questions, not substitutes for the agreement.

## A comparison table

| Question | EoR model | PEO model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the client have a local entity? | Often no, for the target country. | Commonly yes, where the PEO relationship operates. |
| Who is named as the local employer? | The EoR provider or its local employing partner. | The client entity and PEO may share defined responsibilities under a co-employment arrangement. |
| Best fit for | Testing a market, one or more hires, or entering a country without an entity. | An established local business that wants to share or outsource parts of HR and payroll operations. |
| What should be clarified? | Local employment, payroll, benefits, worker classification, and the company’s retained responsibilities. | Co-employment scope, tax and benefit responsibilities, entity obligations, and service boundaries. |

## The decision turns on stage

If the company is hiring one person in a new country and is not ready to establish an entity, an EoR may be the more natural first conversation.

If the company already has a local entity and wants support with payroll, benefits, HR administration, or employer processes, a PEO relationship may be relevant.

Neither model is automatically “more compliant” or “less expensive.” The answer depends on the country, the role, the workforce, the entity, and the work the provider is actually contracted to perform.

## What to ask both providers

Ask for the responsibility map in plain language:

- Who hires and terminates?
- Who owns payroll inputs, tax filings, and corrections?
- Who selects and administers benefits?
- How are employee questions handled?
- Which responsibilities remain with the client company?
- What changes if the relationship, country, or entity changes?

If you cannot explain the model to the employee who will experience it, the model is not clear enough yet.

## The practical conclusion

An EoR is often a bridge into a new country. A PEO is often a support model around an existing local employer. Both can be useful. The right choice is the one whose entity and responsibility structure match the reality of the work.
