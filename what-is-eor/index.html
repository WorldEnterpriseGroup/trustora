EoR 101

What is an Employer of Record?

In plain language: an EoR gives a company a local employment layer in a country where it does not yet have its own entity. For specialist teams, that layer can sit beside the expertise needed to move the work forward.

See the EoR path ↗
A useful starting point for founders, people leaders, and operators asking whether an EoR fits the next hire.

Think of it as the employment layer between ambition and local reality.

Your company still directs the work, manages performance, and builds the relationship with the employee. The EoR is the local employer named in the employment arrangement, coordinating the country-specific employment administration around that person.

That commonly includes contract coordination, payroll, statutory contributions, benefits administration, leave, onboarding, and offboarding. The exact responsibilities depend on the country, the agreement, and the service model.

How the model works

Three relationships. One clearer map.

Good EoR work makes responsibilities visible to the company, the employee, and the local employment partner.

01 / YOUR COMPANY

Owns the work

You set direction, manage the day-to-day, and define what success looks like.

02 / TRUSTORA

Coordinates employment

We help administer the local employment relationship and keep the operating details joined up.

03 / THE EMPLOYEE

Gets a clear experience

The person knows who employs them, how pay and leave work, and where to ask a question.

04 / THE COUNTRY

Sets the context

Local employment, tax, benefits, and worker-protection rules shape the answer.

Operating diagram

The honest version

EoR reduces friction. It does not erase responsibility.

Your company still needs to make good decisions about the role, the working relationship, the offer, and the team. Payroll outsourcing does not automatically transfer every employer obligation, and worker classification still depends on the facts and the applicable rules.
